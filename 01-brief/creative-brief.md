# The creative brief

Fill it in here, in markdown, so the brief sits beside the work it governs and every AI tool reads it. If you drafted it in Word, paste your answers in under each heading. Commit the PDF you turned in to `decks/` with the date in its name.

The nineteen sections from Monday's class, in the same order. What counts is that every section has an answer, not how many pages it fills. Mark every guess with a question mark. For a finished brief to compare against, see [the Resume Place brief](https://meaninglayer.com/handouts/ART-418-example-brief-resume-place.pdf).

Working copy to download and fill in: [CREATIVE-BRIEF-simple.docx](https://meaninglayer.com/handouts/CREATIVE-BRIEF-simple.docx). The longer version, with where each section comes from: [the full template](https://meaninglayer.com/course/business-of-design/handout/creative-brief-template).

*Where each answer comes from:* **You have it** means from a hand-in or your pitch. **Monday** means from the in-class exercises. **Wednesday** means we write it in class. **Before Wednesday** means you draft it on your own.

---

## The brief in one line

GET ____ WHO ____ TO ____ BY ____

*Write it last. The BY is your concept from exercise 2.*

---

## Page one: the situation

### 1. Overview
*You have it: your pitch.* The product in one line, who makes it, who decides, and the timeline.

____

### 2. Problem
*You have it: your problem statement.* Copy it in, word for word.

____

### 3. Goals
*You have it: your pitch.* Three things the work has to achieve, in order.

____

### 4. Audience
*You have it: your audience hand-in.* Who the work leans to, with examples of real people, and what they use today instead.

____

### 5. Insight
*Monday, exercise 1.* One sentence about the person, not the product. Under it: the fact, your guess, and the evidence or the question that would find it.

____

### 6. Competition
*You have it: your research.* The competitors, grouped by kind, and the character each one plays.

____

---

## Page two: the answer

### 7. Message
*You have it: your pitch, sharpened.* What we are trying to say, in one sentence.

____

### 8. Position
*Wednesday.* For ____, ____ is the ____ that ____, because ____.

____

### 9. Character
*Monday, exercise 3.* The character from the twelve, and the path you chose. It should not match your closest competitor's.

____

### 10. Tone
*Monday, your "nevers."* Three things the brand would never do, and a few words for how it sounds.

____

### 11. Channels
*Wednesday.* Where you will reach them, and how the message reads there.

____

### 12. Action and measure
*You have it: your pitch.* The one thing they should do, and the number that tells you it worked.

____

### 13. Principles and mandatories
*Monday, exercise 2.* Two principles: "We learned ____. The implication is that the design should ____." Your concept. Then what must appear and what is off limits.

____

### 14. Open questions
*Every question mark above,* with who or what would answer it, and by when.

____

---

## Page three: what gets built

### 15. Requirements
*You have it: your pitch's features.* "A ____ can ____ so that ____." One per line, marked must, should, or later.

____

### 16. Site map and main steps
*Before Wednesday.* The pages or screens, then the steps from the home page to the one thing you want them to do.

____

### 17. Content
*Before Wednesday.* What each page needs, who writes it, and who keeps it current.

____

### 18. Technology
*Before Wednesday.* What it is built on, what that costs, and why.

____

### 19. Out of scope
*Before Wednesday.* What this version will not do.

____
